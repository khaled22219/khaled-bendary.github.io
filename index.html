About

I am a development economist studying how public policies and migration opportunities reshape household decisions and human capital accumulation in developing countries. My research examines how changes in economic opportunities alter intra-household bargaining power and the allocation of resources, particularly time, education, and work. I am interested in both the welfare effects of these changes and their distribution within households: who gains, who bears the adjustment costs, and how gender norms and unequal outside options shape these responses. Using quasi-experimental methods and field experiments, I study these questions across settings involving social protection, educational migration, and women’s economic opportunities, with particular attention to consequences for children’s schooling, child labor, child marriage, and women’s labor force participation.

Work in Progress

When cash meets norms: Evaluating the impact of Egypt's Takaful program on attitudes towards domestic violence

Solo-authored

Abstract

Do cash transfers to women empower them, or provoke the men around them? I study Egypt’s Takaful program, which pays its benefits primarily to women, and ask whether it shifts the norms that justify intimate partner violence. Eligibility runs on a proxy means test that the government disclosed but household surveys do not contain. I recover the assignment formula from archived government pages and reconstruct the score in survey data with a random forest. This allows a fuzzy regression discontinuity design at the eligibility threshold. After roughly eight years of exposure, men are likely to show more accepting attitudes towards violence, as measured by a five-scenario index standard in the DHS. The pattern is consistent with a male-status-threat channel: when transfers make women’s outside options visible, acceptance of violence increases rather than falls. The outcome is attitudes, not reported violence, but the result shows that targeting transfers to women can carry an unintended normative cost.
