About

I am a development economist working in the economics of education, family and migration. In my research, I am interested in understanding barriers to human capital accumulation in developing world using quasi-experimental methods and field experiments. In particular, the impact of exposure to public policies and migration shocks on household welfare, including education attainment, child labor, child marriage, and female labor participation, mediated via intra-household allocation of resources (time use and substitution effects) and bargaining power (costs of adjustment argos: who gains and who loses). In many contexts, these constraints are more pronounced for women or families of migrants' background, hence gender-, family- and migration-specific aspects of these questions are a natural part of my research identity.
